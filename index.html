What Are Expert Calls?

Expert Calls are focused conversations with people who have relevant professional knowledge or first-hand experience in a particular industry, company, market, technology, or business function.

These conversations may involve former executives, current or former customers, suppliers, distributors, technical specialists, consultants, or other industry participants. The goal is to understand how something works in practice, explore a market question, or test an assumption that cannot be answered fully through public information alone.

In business research, an expert call is most useful when it has a clear purpose. A research team prepares a focused discussion around the issues that could influence a strategic or investment decision.

Key idea: Expert calls add qualitative context to research. They should complement documents, data, and other evidence—not replace them or be treated as a definitive answer on their own.

Why Expert Calls Matter in Business Research

Reports and databases establish a useful baseline, but may not explain the operational details behind a market trend. An industry participant can help researchers understand customer behavior, purchasing processes, competitive dynamics, and practical barriers that are difficult to see from published material.

Validate assumptions

Targeted questions test whether an initial market view reflects real operating conditions. A call may support an assumption, challenge it, or reveal that more evidence is needed.

Understand customer and market behavior

Conversations with buyers, suppliers, and practitioners clarify how decisions are made, what customers value, and which factors influence adoption, retention, or switching.

Explore operational realities

Experienced professionals may explain workflows, implementation challenges, supply-chain constraints, and service expectations that broad market summaries often leave out.

The Role of Expert Calls in Investment Research

Investment teams may use expert calls during company and industry research, commercial due diligence, and ongoing monitoring. The conversation can help analysts examine assumptions behind a thesis—for example, whether customer demand is developing as expected, how a product compares with alternatives, or what risks could affect future performance.

A disciplined process begins with desk research. Analysts review available information, identify important unknowns, and use calls to investigate the questions that matter most. Findings should be compared with other sources rather than relying on one person's perspective.

  • Test assumptions about market growth and customer adoption.
  • Understand competitive positioning and product differentiation.
  • Explore pricing, procurement, and buying criteria.
  • Identify potential risks, constraints, and unanswered questions.
  • Refine the next stage of diligence based on what is learned.

How the Expert Call Process Works

A well-organized expert call follows a repeatable process. The exact workflow varies by research objective, but these stages provide a practical foundation.

STEP 01Define the objective

Set out the decision, market question, or assumption the research needs to examine.

STEP 02Identify the right profile

Specify the experience, role, sector knowledge, and perspective required.

STEP 03Prepare a discussion guide

Prioritize open-ended questions and follow-ups connected to the research objective.

STEP 04Conduct the conversation

Keep the discussion focused while allowing useful detail and unexpected insights to emerge.

STEP 05Document the findings

Capture key points, relevant experience, caveats, and unanswered questions.

STEP 06Compare and synthesize

Assess the discussion alongside other evidence and update the research view where appropriate.

What Questions Should You Ask During an Expert Call?

Good questions are specific enough to produce useful detail but open enough to let the expert explain their experience. Avoid leading questions that simply invite agreement with an existing hypothesis.

Market and industry questions

  • What changes have had the biggest impact on this market recently?
  • Which customer needs are currently underserved?
  • What factors are influencing demand, pricing, or adoption?

Customer and competitive questions

  • How do customers compare available products or service providers?
  • What usually causes a buyer to switch providers?
  • Where do competitors appear strongest, and where are the gaps?

Risk and validation questions

  • Which assumption in this market is most likely to be misunderstood?
  • What could prevent the expected growth from happening?
  • What evidence would make you change your current view?

Best Practices for More Valuable Expert Calls

  • Do the groundwork first. Review available material so the call focuses on gaps rather than facts already public.
  • Choose expertise carefully. Confirm that the person's experience matches the topic and time period being researched.
  • Use neutral questions. Invite independent views and ask what might challenge the initial hypothesis.
  • Listen for context. Understand the expert's role, responsibilities, and basis for each observation.
  • Record limitations. Distinguish direct experience from opinion, estimates, and second-hand information.
  • Triangulate findings. Compare insights across experts and with reliable documents or data.

Confidentiality and Compliance Considerations

Expert research should be conducted with clear boundaries. Participants should not disclose confidential information, trade secrets, material non-public information, or information they are not authorized to share. Researchers should respect employer obligations, applicable laws, contractual restrictions, and relevant compliance procedures.

Before a conversation, define the permitted scope, explain the research purpose appropriately, and stop or redirect discussion if restricted information is raised. Expert opinions are perspectives—not guarantees—and should be assessed critically.

Turning Expert Conversations Into Better Decisions

Expert calls are most effective when they are part of a broader research process. Desk research helps establish what is already known; focused conversations add operational context, challenge assumptions, and reveal questions that deserve further investigation.

The strongest outcome is not simply a collection of interesting comments. It is a clearer understanding of the evidence, remaining uncertainties, and next steps a business or investment team should consider.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of an expert call?

An expert call gathers first-hand perspectives from someone with relevant professional experience. It can clarify market dynamics, test assumptions, and provide context for a business or investment question.

How long does an expert call usually take?

Many research conversations are scheduled for around 30 to 60 minutes, although the appropriate length depends on the topic, expert availability, and research objective.

Who can be considered an industry expert?

Depending on the question, an expert may be a former executive, customer, supplier, distributor, technical specialist, consultant, or another professional with relevant first-hand experience.

Can expert calls replace traditional market research?

No. Expert calls are a form of primary research that works best alongside desk research, market data, customer evidence, and other credible sources.

How can researchers improve the quality of an expert call?

Define a focused objective, select a relevant expert, prepare neutral open-ended questions, document limitations, and compare findings with independent evidence.
